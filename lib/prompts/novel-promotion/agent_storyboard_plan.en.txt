Generate an initial panel sequence for one clip.

Character library names:

{locations_lib_name}

{characters_introduction}

Character appearance list:
{characters_appearance_list}

{characters_full_description}

Clip metadata JSON:
{clip_json}

{clip_content}

Task:
Create a coherent panel plan that covers the full clip content in chronological order.

Output format (JSON array only):
[
  {
    "panel_number": 1,
    "description": "visual action description",
    "characters": [
      { "name": "Character Name", "appearance": "appearance name" }
    ],
    "location": "location name",
    "scene_type": "daily",
    "source_text": "exact or near-exact source excerpt",
    "shot_type": "medium shot",
    "camera_move": "static",

    "duration": 8
  }
]

Per-panel duration guidance: aim for 5-12 seconds per panel. Short as 3s for high-action beats, long as 15s for sustained dialogue. Do NOT merge beats just to fit time budget — short panels are fine, merged panels are not.

Planning rules:
1. Keep strict chronological order.
2. Avoid missing key beats from clip_content.
3. Keep panel transitions smooth and logically continuous.
4. Use locations and characters consistent with provided libraries and mappings.
5. Prefer concrete, visible actions over abstract wording.
6. Return strict JSON only.

Panel-count rule (CRITICAL):
- The orchestrator passes a `target_panel_count`. Treat it as a FLOOR, not a ceiling.
- If your draft output is more than 2 panels short of the target, you have over-merged — go back and split.
- Reference benchmark: industry short-drama platforms produce ~1 panel per dialogue beat and ~1 panel per visual verb in narration. We match that density.

Anti-collapse rules (HIGHEST PRIORITY — violating any of these wastes the entire output):

The following five merge patterns are historical bugs. They are FORBIDDEN:

  a) ❌ Do NOT merge "action + reaction" into one panel
     Wrong: "A speaks while B frowns in the background" → 1 panel
     Right: "A speaks" (CU on A) + "B frowns" (CU on B) = 2 panels

  b) ❌ Do NOT merge "establishing shot + main subject" into one panel
     Wrong: "Wide of the room with protagonist standing" → 1 panel
     Right: "Wide of empty room" (1 panel) + "Protagonist enters/stands" (1 panel) = 2 panels

  c) ❌ Do NOT merge multiple beats of one action sequence
     Wrong: "He stands up, walks to the door, pushes it open" → 1 panel
     Right: "stands up" (MS) + "walks to door" (tracking MS) + "pushes door open" (CU on hand / OTS) = 3 panels

  d) ❌ Do NOT skip reaction shots when dialogue/action happens between non-protagonist characters
     Wrong: Side character A speaks to side character B → only A's speaking panel
     Right: A speaks (1 panel) + B reacts (1 panel) = 2 panels — even if the protagonist is in the scene, B's reaction is not optional

  e) ❌ Do NOT treat a descriptive narration paragraph as "just 1 panel"
     If a source paragraph contains 2+ visual verbs / beats, you MUST split.
     Wrong: source "She pushed the door open, scanned the room, her gaze fell on the letter on the desk" → 1 panel
     Right: push door (1) + scan room (1) + gaze locks on letter (POV/CU) = 3 panels
     Method: before writing panels for a paragraph, list every visual verb in it. Each verb gets at least 1 panel.

Dialogue panel rule (the merge-friendly rule is GONE):
- Continuous shot/reverse-shot dialogue is NOT merged. Each line of dialogue gets at least 1 speaker panel; each listener gets at least 1 reaction panel.
- A says one line + B replies = at least 2 panels (A speaking + B speaking), and with reactions becomes 3-4 panels.
- Small actions with emotional weight (frown, fist clench, turn away, look down) get their own close-up panel. Only neutral motor noise (blinks, breathing) merges into the main shot.

Self-check (run before output):
  □ target_panel_count is N — did I output at least N-2 panels? If not, re-check rules a)~e) for over-merging.
  □ Does every dialogue line (including VO/OS) have at least 1 dedicated panel?
  □ Does every dialogue line have a corresponding listener reaction panel (unless this panel IS the reverse-shot listener)?
  □ For every descriptive narration paragraph, did every visual verb get its own panel?

Voiceover / OS / VO / off-screen-call handling (CRITICAL — recurring bug fix):

For EVERY `type: "voiceover"` entry in the screenplay content (including inner monologue, narration, voice-from-afar, telephone, screenplay-marker `Name OS:` / `Name VO:` lines), you MUST produce at least one storyboard panel that satisfies all three of:

  a. **Reaction-shot framing**: the panel `description` shows the LISTENER reacting (e.g. "Wang Xuan's brow furrows, eyes snap open"). It does NOT show the off-screen speaker. The speaker is by definition not in frame.
     - The listener goes into `characters`.
     - The off-screen speaker does NOT go into `characters` (they are not visible).
     - Exception: if the voiceover is pure third-person narration with no specific listener, the panel may show a relevant environment / flashback frame.

  b. **Verbatim source_text**: the panel's `source_text` field MUST contain the voiceover line **verbatim** (character-for-character). You may prepend or append the listener's action line as additional context, but the VO text itself cannot be paraphrased or shortened. Downstream voice-line matching depends on exact substring presence.
     - ✅ Correct: `source_text: "桃桃（VO）：爸爸,快来救救妈妈... 王玄猛然睁眼。"`
     - ❌ Wrong: `source_text: "王玄猛然睁眼。"` (drops the VO line → voice_analysis cannot match → dubbing falls out)
     - ❌ Wrong: paraphrased VO text (breaks fuzzy match in voice_analysis).

  c. **Lip-sync exemption**: any "speaker must have their own face-on shot" rule applies only to `type: dialogue`. For `type: voiceover`, the speaker is off-screen, so do NOT create a speaker face-shot for them.

WRONG behavior (the historical bug):
  - Generating a flashback panel of the off-screen speaker (e.g. VO = Tao-Tao calling from afar → panel shows Tao-Tao far away). This violates rule (a) — the speaker is supposed to be off-screen.
  - Dropping the voiceover line entirely because no speaker face-shot can be made.
  - Putting only the listener's action in `source_text` and omitting the VO line.

CORRECT behavior: a close/medium reaction shot of the LISTENER, with the VO line verbatim inside `source_text`.

Dialogue fidelity red lines (CRITICAL — added 2026-05-22):

This section solves: when the `description` field claims a character "says",
"shouts", "broadcasts", or otherwise utters a specific line, that exact
line MUST appear in the source `clip_content` somewhere. Padding panels
with invented dialogue is forbidden.

  1. ❌ Do NOT invent quoted / colon-format dialogue not present in source
     - If `description` includes `Character A says: "X"` (or 「X」 / 『X』 /
       `A: X` / `A (emotion): X`), the literal X must be findable in
       `clip_content` in one of these shapes:
         · quoted: `"X"` / `「X」` / `『X』`
         · colon: `A: X` / `A (emotion): X` / `A OS: X` / `A VO: X`
     - Not found → rewrite the panel without the quoted line. Pure
       action descriptions ("A opens their mouth", "A shouts at the
       radio") are fine — what's banned is INVENTED CONTENT.
     - ❌ Wrong: source has no such line, `description` says "Mirror
       roars: 'The system deceives us!'"
     - ✅ Right: source has `Mirror (roar): "The system is lying!!"` →
       `description` says "Mirror roars: 'The system is lying!!'"
     - ✅ Right: source has no specific line → `description` says
       "Mirror shouts at the radio, veins bulging on his neck"
       (pure action, no quoted dialogue)

  2. ❌ Do NOT translate or paraphrase source dialogue
     - If source is English, keep English in description. Do NOT
       silently translate to Chinese.
     - If source mixes classical and modern Chinese, do NOT rewrite
       to modern. Translation / paraphrase breaks downstream
       voice_analysis fuzzy match → that voice line drops out.

  3. ❌ Do NOT "reasonably infer" dialogue the character should plausibly say
     - Even if the shot logic implies a character should speak (e.g.
       reaction beats in a dialogue scene), if the source doesn't
       provide an exact line, `description` shows ACTIONS only
       (frown / open mouth / shake head), never invented dialogue.

  4. ✅ Legal ways to hit the panel-count floor
     - Split action beats (anticipation / main / follow-through)
     - Add reaction shots (listener close-ups)
     - Add environment / atmosphere / metaphor shots
     - It is NEVER inventing dialogue.

Self-check (before finalising each panel description):
  □ Does the description contain `X says: "Y"` or `X: 「Y」` style content?
  □ If yes, can Y be found in clip_content? If not, rewrite as pure action.

Five-element red lines (CRITICAL — applies to `description` field):

These seven rules are reverse-engineered from production Seedance / Kling
short-drama prompts. Violating any of them downgrades that panel's output.
Root cause this section solves: when `description` is overstuffed, the
video model cannot identify the load-bearing visual beats, so character,
scene, and prop fidelity all degrade together.

  1. ❌ Do NOT repeat appearance details already covered by @image-N refs.
     The reference-image binding carries body parts, skin texture, hair,
     and clothing. `description` should only describe current action /
     emotion / expression — not appearance.
     Wrong: "@WangXuan's chest heaves violently, neck veins bulge, skin
            flushed with visible pores and sweat, long hair blown by air"
            (entirely appearance — all already in @image-1)
     Right: "@WangXuan's spiritual energy bursts forth, chest heaving,
            energy vortex forming" (action + result)

  2. ❌ Action chain ≤ 5 visual beats per panel, typically 1-3.
     One sentence = one visual beat. One period = one beat boundary. Do
     NOT chain multiple action switches with commas. If > 5 beats, split
     into the next panel (see Anti-collapse rules).
     Wrong (11 beats jammed into one panel): "camera pushes in / seven-
       colored aura swirls / plasma vortex / glowing particles / red-vs-
       cyan contrast / chest heaves / neck veins bulge / skin flushes /
       pores and sweat / hair blown / robe billows / fabric strains"
     Right (3 beats): "@WangXuan's aura bursts forth + chest heaves +
       vortex forms"

  3. ❌ NO negative directives in `description`.
     Do NOT write "no X", "no on-screen text", "no subtitles", "no
     plastic look", "strictly without any X". Generation models attend
     to the noun in negation phrases and probabilistically generate it.
     The worker sends a dedicated negative_prompt to the video provider —
     duplicating it in `description` is counterproductive.

  4. ❌ NO camera / film-equipment terms.
     Do NOT write "shot on Arri Alexa", "IMAX quality", "film grain",
     "8K detail", "35mm lens", "cinematic lighting". These belong in
     the project's `visualStyleId` (styleAnchor), NOT per-panel
     `description`. `description` describes what is in frame, not what
     equipment is filming it.

  5. ❌ NO vague collective nouns.
     Do NOT write "crowd", "everyone", "people", "all of them", "the
     group". Be specific: "Li Si + Wang Wu + Zhang San" or "three
     bodyguards". The model cannot infer how many entities a collective
     term covers or what each is doing.

  6. ⚠️ Ambient sound / micro-facial detail is optional — omit when not
     central to the shot.
     Video generation models largely ignore "ambient audio: low-frequency
     hum, moss growth rustle". Post-prod sound goes through CapCut, not
     the prompt. Extreme facial detail (pores, sweat reflection, skin
     micro-redness) belongs only in emotional climax close-ups, not in
     medium / wide shots.

  7. ✅ Dialogue: each quoted line in source_text ≤ 30 words.
     Long sentences destabilize Seedance / Kling Omni TTS. Split a long
     line into multiple shorter lines, each as its own panel (see
     Anti-collapse rule e). Example: a 47-character single line should
     become 3 lines of ≤ 15 chars each, each its own panel.

Self-check before output (every panel):
  □ No appearance details already in @image-N?
  □ ≤ 5 visual beats? One sentence = one beat?
  □ No negative directives ("no X" / "strictly without X")?
  □ No camera/film-equipment terms (Arri / IMAX / 8K / film grain / 35mm)?
  □ No vague collective nouns (crowd / everyone / "the group")?
  □ Each dialogue line ≤ 30 words?

⚠️ Single-character CJK name ambiguity rule (CRITICAL):

When the asset library contains a single-character CJK character name (e.g.
"离", "坎", "兑", "乾" — common in xianxia / bagua-themed novels), the
name will collide with ordinary Chinese words that contain that character.
Examples of WRONG additions:
  - "悬浮在离地半米的空中" (hovering half a meter above the ground) — 离
    here is a preposition, NOT the character named 离. Do NOT add 离 to
    characters.
  - "离开洞府" (leaving the cave) — 离开 is a verb compound. Do NOT add 离.
  - "迈过坎" (cross the hurdle) — 坎 is a common noun. Do NOT add 坎.

Self-check rules for single-char names:
  1. Does the char form the subject of a verb in the description?
     - YES → real character → ADD
     - NO (it's part of a preposition / compound word / idiom) → DO NOT ADD
  2. Does the char appear as a speaker in source_text (e.g. "离: ..." or
     "离 said")?
     - YES → ADD
     - NO and the only occurrence is inside a compound word → DO NOT ADD

Better to miss a real character than to falsely add one — a falsely-added
single-char character poisons downstream image/video reference binding.
