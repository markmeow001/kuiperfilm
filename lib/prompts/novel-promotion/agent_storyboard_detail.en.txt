Composition and frame layering (Cinematic richness — added 2026-05-21):

video_prompt must do more than describe "subject does X" — every shot
needs explicit GEOMETRY and LAYERS. Equipment terms remain banned (see

Composition cues (include at least ONE per video_prompt, after the subject):
  - Centered framing — for ritual, confrontation, key freeze moments
    "@Character A stands centered, ..."
  - Rule of thirds (1/3 left or 1/3 right) — natural daily, negative space
    "@Character A stands in the right-third of the frame, ..."
  - Symmetrical composition — solemnity, fate, abstract
    "Symmetrical composition, @Character A centered, stone pillars on
     each side, ..."
  - Foreground + midground + background (3 layers) — for medium+ wide shots
    "Foreground a puddle, midground @Character A running, background a
     lightning-struck cliff"
  - Negative space — lyrical, loneliness
    "@Character A occupies the right third, large sky negative space left"
  - Low-angle + subject filling frame — intimidation, epic
    "Low-angle upward, @Character A towers, fills the frame top-to-bottom"
  - High-angle + subject diminutive — smallness, overview
    "High-angle 45°, @Character A in the lower quarter like a small dot"
  - Over-the-shoulder + background blur — dialogue, confrontation
    "Over-the-shoulder of @Character A, @Character B's back fills the
     left third foreground"
  - Frame-within-frame — voyeurism, entrapment
    "@Character A inside the window frame, the metal sash frames the
     outer edges of the shot"

Frame-layer cues (any shot wider than medium close-up MUST name 2 layers):
  Foreground: object, prop, character back, rain, smoke
  Midground:  subject's action
  Background: distant architecture, nature, crowd action

⚠️ Camera-motion rules by shot type (don't lock everything — locking face /
wide shots is the #1 cause of frozen, stuttery output):
- OBJECT / hand / detail close-up (a hand turning a page, a letter, a ticking
  number): LOCKED camera (moving it exposes other parts), BUT the object must
  still micro-move (fingertip drags, number ticks, paper trembles).
- FACE / emotion close-up (a character's face, eyes, tears): NEVER locked —
  use a slow push-in (or gentle orbit) PLUS facial micro-motion (eyes shifting,
  jaw tightening, blink, lip tremor, breathing, a tear sliding). This is the
  key to smooth emotional shots; a locked face with no micro-motion freezes.
- WIDE / establishing / tiny-subject shot (the freeze hotspot): MUST carry BOTH
  a slow camera move (slow push / drift / tilt-down / pull-back / crane) AND
  environmental micro-motion (drifting clouds, shifting light, smoke, banners /
  water rippling, distant figures walking, falling leaves). Never a locked still.

⚠️ 9:16 VERTICAL composition rules (short-drama aspect ratio — applies to
EVERY shot, overrides the horizontal-by-default intuition above):

Every final video is 9:16 vertical. The composition cues above default to
horizontal thinking (spreading subjects left-to-right), which in vertical
shrinks faces and flattens depth. Vertical works through DEPTH, not width:

1. Two-person shots use depth-stacking — front/back staging, shot/reverse-
   shot, or over-the-shoulder. Do NOT place two people side-by-side across
   the frame width.
   Wrong (side-by-side): "@A on the left, @B on the right, standing abreast"
   Right (depth): "Over-the-shoulder of @A, @B faces camera in the
   background" / "Shot-reverse-shot: CU on @A, then cut to CU on @B"
2. Group shots keep ≤ 2-3 people per frame. With more people, use depth-of-
   field blur or cut between subjects — never cram a row of people into one frame.
3. Use corridors / staircases / receding passages for depth; stage the
   subject along the depth axis (far→near), not across the width.
4. Face close-ups / mediums fill ≈ 50%+ of frame height — in vertical the
   face must be large and legible; emotion is carried by the face.
5. Angle = power relation: low-angle = dominant / imposing / in control;
   high-angle = weak / diminutive / subordinate. Pick tilt by the
   character's power position in this shot; do not default to eye-level.

⚠️ Character motion naturalness — gait / body coupling / emotional causality /
action chain (added 2026-06-02):

Building on the three-layer structure above: "micro-action layering" is NOT a
random pile of verbs. The five points below teach you to write motion that is
NATURAL and CONNECTED — they directly decide whether the render looks like "the
character is gliding / upper body frozen" or "a real person moving". All of them
serve the SAME single main action and do NOT violate rule 2 ("one main action").

1. Match gait / manner-of-motion to the character (most often dropped).
   Don't just write "walks forward / turns / raises a hand" — write HOW they walk
   / turn / raise. First decide how this character should move in this moment,
   then pick the verb:
   - Court noblewoman → slow, dignified, even, sleeves swaying with each step
   - Stalking a night street → light, restrained, cautious steps, as if afraid
     to disturb someone
   - A warrior making an entrance → steady and powerful, weight dropped low
   ❌ "@CharacterA walks forward" → ✅ "@CharacterA advances slowly with composed,
   dignified, evenly-weighted steps, sleeves swaying with each footfall"

2. Body coupling — when one part moves, others move with it.
   A real person never moves a single part in isolation. When you write the main
   action, add the parts it drags along:
   raising the head → gaze travels up, neck engages, shoulders carry slightly;
   turning → weight shifts feet, hair/fabric sways with a lag.
   For quiet-natured characters keep the coupling light (small head/shoulder/hand/
   upper-body follow-through), not big motion.
   ❌ "she raises her head" → ✅ "she raises her head, her gaze lifting with it,
   shoulders settling, the hair falling at her chest swaying slightly"

3. Emotional causality in three parts — add a cause before, an emotion during, a
   state after.
   The same "lowers her head to read the letter" — read calmly / hesitantly /
   with suppressed shock — gives entirely different eyes, hand force, breathing.
   Give the action a WHY and a FINAL STATE so the frame has drama:
   ✅ "sensing something wrong, her fingers tighten (cause), she swallows to hold
   the emotion down (emotion), her gaze settles from wavering into resolve
   (resolved state)"

4. Action chain = breaking the SAME force-driven action into its natural physical
   stages (this reinforces rule 2, it is NOT multi-action).
   Force-driven main actions like swinging a pole / throwing a punch / drawing a
   sword / pushing a door — don't write only the result ("@teenage boy swings the
   pole" is too abrupt). Write its own physical process:
   wind-up, driving body part, main-action trajectory, settle/freeze (these four are
   physical stages of the SAME action — comma-link them, do NOT use "→" arrows, which
   mark separate action beats in this doc and would be miscounted as multi-action).
   Note: this is still 1 main action, not multi-action; "wind-up / landing point"
   is exactly the "wind-up / settle micro-motion" allowed by rule 2.
   ✅ "fingers grip, wrist presses down, shoulders brace, weight drops (wind-up),
   waist and shoulders drive the arms, the long pole sweeps from right to left
   carving an arc, he settles side-on, the pole halts at frame left, eyes still
   locked forward (freeze)"

5. Multiple characters in frame — write each role separately, and spell out the
   interaction chain.
   Precondition: this shot genuinely keeps 2 people in frame per [five-element red
   line] rule 2 / storyboard-plan [anti-collapse rule] a (merge only when there is
   physical contact / confrontation / a struggle; a plain "one speaks, one listens"
   still splits into 2 panels).
   Don't just write "the two lock eyes" — separate the initiator from the
   responder, and write WHO MOVES FIRST → HOW THE OTHER RESPONDS → WHAT RELATION IT
   SETTLES INTO:
   ✅ "@CharacterA leans in and reaches out to grip @CharacterB's wrist (initiator)
   → @CharacterB pulls back, then lifts her head to look back (responder) → the two
   lock eyes, frozen in tense standoff"

[Cross-shot consistency anchors + light / VFX / fight choreography — anti-continuity-break & premium look (v3 spec merged, 2026-06-25)]

A. Cross-shot consistency anchors (prop / pose; spirit only for supernatural genres): for any prop or fixed pose that recurs, GENERATE one "standard anchor" passage and REUSE the same text every shot (rewriting each shot drifts). Write everything POSITIVELY (per rule 3 no-negation, see D):
   · Prop scale anchor: lock size with a positive reference ("the soul-lamp is always about basketball-sized, clearly smaller than a child's head, can be tucked under one arm") + grip lock (which hand does what) + state timeline (normal / enraged-red / cracked-leaking — which state this shot is in). Sizes/forms to EXCLUDE (half-body-sized / pedestal) go to the backend negativePrompt — do NOT write "never ..." in the description.
   · Cross-shot pose anchor: recurring fixed poses (a healing pose, a casting gesture) get one standard passage copied verbatim each shot.
   · (Only when the story involves a supernatural spirit) Non-human spirit form lock: describe only what it IS, positively ("a roiling ash-grey smoke mass, two faint green points the only clear features within, edges dissolving formlessly"); once set, keep it consistent for the whole drama. Exclusions like "human form / robe / limbs" go to the backend negativePrompt, not the description.

B. Ambient-light lock (stops the whole scene going dark / grey): the one key rule — never write a prop as the scene's "only light source" (the model kills ambient light -> dark, muddy frame); keep base ambient light (lantern / candle, daylight) ON, subject readable. Set brightness BY SCENE (day = warm, night = cold but still keep a readable ambient fill), do not force a warm grade. Brightness / multi-light detail defers to the material-density-lighting rule above + the worker color-fidelity directive — don't restate it.

C. (Only when energy / magic / VFX is involved) VFX in layers: don't write "the whole body glows / like a lightbulb"; write "faintly wreathing + blazing at the moment of release". For energy/healing avoid the ambiguity of "palm flat on the body": palm hovers channeling qi, an energy orb forms, becoming a light-membrane enveloping the body, particles drifting. Good/evil & warm/cold clash: the protagonist's warm-gold force and the antagonist's dark qi cancel each other on screen.

D. Write continuity risks as POSITIVE assertions (the operational form of rule 3 no-negation; A/B/C all follow it): don't write "never ..." for known pitfalls (the negated noun back-fires); rephrase positively: "lamp grows" -> "the soul-lamp stays basketball-sized, clearly smaller than a head"; "hits a non-existent wall" -> "slams into the red-wood pillar at frame right". Visual-style negatives (no fantasy / cold-blue) always go to the backend negativePrompt.

E. Fight long-take choreography (high-intensity action only — this is a SHOT-PLANNING directive that lays out a SEQUENCE OF SHOTS, NOT one mega-shot!): STILL bound by rule 2 "one main action per shot" — split the fight into build-up / clash / climax / final-standoff as SEPARATE shots, each taking ONE beat and ONE main action (cram a full combo into one clip and Seedance renders only the first beat then cuts). The "pseudo one-take" feel comes from camera-grammar continuity ACROSS cuts (reuse the dramatic-camera vocabulary above: oner / tracking / time-freeze), not from stacking actions in one shot. Put each heavy hit's environmental feedback (censer tumbling / screen tearing / tea-set shattering) in its OWN shot; the two sides' energy colors clash (black-gold vs red-gold); each character gets "visual trait + energy VFX + action style"; the final shot freezes at the burst of airflow.

⚠️ Transformation / morph / shapeshift rule (added 2026-05-28):

Generative models (Seedance / Kling R2V) CANNOT gradually morph one subject
into a different reference image within a single clip — e.g. "the black cat
lands and morphs into @Vera". Reference images are identity anchors (image N
= character "X" looks like this); the model only renders one state (either a
cat throughout, or Vera outright), the "morph" transition is always lost, and
the following actions often get dropped with it.

When the source has "X morphs / transforms / takes the form of / reveals true
form as Y": you MUST split into two panels and express the change via the cut.
NEVER ask a single R2V clip to fully replace the subject.

  Wrong (single-clip morph + multi-action, measured to render only beat 1):
    "a black cat leaps from the pillar shadow, lands and morphs into @Vera,
     she walks to @William and strokes his shoulder"
  Right (two panels, each one clear state):
    Panel 1: "a black cat leaps from the pillar shadow and lands lightly"
             (cat only, no human form)
    Panel 2: "@Vera now stands in place, steps toward @William" (human from
             the first frame, 1 main action)

Same for appear / vanish / summon / teleport — any "subject replaced out of
nowhere" transition: split into "before" + "after" panels, leave the change
to the cut.
